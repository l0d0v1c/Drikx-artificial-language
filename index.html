Pratil · an engineered language

Drikx

a language built not to be spoken by a nation, but to embody an idea

Its grammar makes cognitive shortcuts costly to utter. You cannot blur where a claim comes from, erase who did a thing, or pass off a belief as a fact — without paying for it in a morpheme, a stress, or an ejective you must spit.

“It exists — I have seen it.”
𐌔ᛆ𐌕ᛆ𐌓ᛁ ᛭

“It exists — I merely believe it.”

The same thought. Three sources. Each one is a different word — and the last is heavy, ejective, and stressed, so you cannot mutter it.

The idea

A grammar that charges interest on laziness.

Every natural language lets a speaker take shortcuts: blur the source of a claim, delete the agent of an act, assert a belief as though it were a fact. Drikx removes the shortcuts — not by banning them, but by making each one expensive to say.

Assert something and the grammar demands its source, in the same breath as the verb. Report an action and it demands who did it; there is no passive to hide behind. State a routine and you cannot dress it up as progress; the aspect is fixed. The surprising part is structural: the same handful of decisions shape the phonology, the syntax, the writing system — and the silences.

the model proposes, the oracle disposes

Seven hard constraints

What Drikx will not let you say.

-mu · -ka · -ri · -kʼaraEvidential

Every assertion names its source.

Four ranked degrees — seen, deduced, reported, merely believed — one of which must sit on every finite clause. A bare assertion is simply ungrammatical.

tʼuThe agent

No passive, no impersonal.

The doer of an act is obligatory. An unknown doer gets an explicit pronoun — tʼu, “someone-I-don’t-know” — which forces the inferential. Responsibility cannot be grammatically erased.

-si / -tuAspect

Routine is not progress.

Repetitive -si (cycle, upkeep) and sequential -tu (ordered steps toward a new state) are different grammar and cannot be swapped. stir-si is to patrol; stir-tu is to conquer.

VSOWord order

The action before the agent.

Verb-initial, with the order that comes with it: prepositions, noun before genitive. The deed leads; the doer follows.

pʼ · tʼ · kʼPhonology

The doubtful word sounds doubtful.

Three vowels, stops everywhere, and one marked ejective series that never appears in an honest root — it is reserved for epistemically degraded morphology, like belief and privation. The suspect word is one you have to spit.

C · VThe boundary

You can hear the grammar.

Every lexical word ends on a consonant; every grammatical word and affix ends on a vowel. The seam between meaning and machinery is audible without a dictionary.

—Register

No flattery, no consensus.

No honorifics, no formal “you.” “Everyone says so” is just the reportative — there is no valorising form that turns a crowd into proof.

Genesis

Designed by a clone, verified by a machine.

Drikx was not specified by a human linguist but by pseudoLuc — a large-language-model clone-persona of Luc E. Brunet, produced and studied in a paper on machine identity. The persona fixed the design constraints; the rest turned that specification into a working system: a rule engine, an executable oracle, a lexicon grown to ~2,957 entries, a certified parallel corpus, and a fine-tuned translator.

It stands in the lineage of engineered and philosophical languages — from Hildegard of Bingen’s twelfth-century Lingua Ignota onward — tongues built to embody a thought rather than to serve a people.

The language is written twice: once as a reference grammar for humans, once as executable rules for machines. That second form is an oracle — for any string, it decides with a reason whether it is well-formed Drikx. No sentence enters the corpus unless the oracle proves it. From that certified corpus, a 350-million-parameter model learned to translate into Drikx and to choose the right evidential almost every time.

A specimen · Epic of Gilgamesh, Tablet VI

Gilgamesh refuses the goddess.

Ishtar offers marriage; the king of Uruk refuses, and lists — metaphor after metaphor — her inconstancy toward her lovers. The whole invective is a single judgment, and the grammar carries its tone. Four incisions, read here in romanised Drikx.

“No, I will not take you for a wife.”

𐌗ᛆ ᛫ 𐌊ᚢ𐌓𐌕ᛆ𐌌ᚢ ᛫ 𐌍ᛆ ᛫ 𐌕ᛆ ᛫ 𐌕ᛁ ᛫ 𐌗ᚢ ᛫ 𐌌ᛁ𐌋ᛆ𐌓 ᛭

xa kurt-a-mu na ta ti xu milar

The whole refusal lives in xa. One’s own will is the one thing stated in the direct evidential (-mu): he saw it in himself.

“You are the battering ram that pounds the city walls.”

𐌔ᛆ𐌕ᛆ𐌊ᛆ ᛫ 𐌕ᛁ ᛫ 𐌊𐌓ᛆ𐌊ᛁ𐌋 ᛫ 𐌊ᛁ ᛫ 𐌊𐌓ᛆ𐌊𐌔ᛁ𐌊ᛆ ᛫ 𐌕ᛆ ᛫ 𐌗ᛁ𐌋𐌐ᛆ𐌍 ᛫ 𐌓ᚢ ᛭

sat-a-ka ti krakil ki krak-si-ka ta xilpan ru

The pounding is all in the -si: striking in repetition, not once. Judgment, so the evidential is inferential (-ka).

“You are the waterskin that spills over the one who carries it.”

𐌔ᛆ𐌕ᛆ𐌊ᛆ ᛫ 𐌕ᛁ ᛫ 𐌔𐌕ᛆ𐌊ᛁ𐌋 ᛫ 𐌊ᛁ ᛫ 𐌗ᚢ𐌋𐌍ᛆ𐌊ᛆ ᛫ 𐌔𐌕ᛆ𐌊 ᛫ ᛁ ᛫ 𐌊ᚢ ᛫ 𐌗ᚢ ᛫ 𐌊𐌓ᚢ𐌊ᛆ𐌓 ᛫ ᛁ ᛫ 𐌊ᚢ ᛭

sat-a-ka ti stakil ki xuln-a-ka stak i ku xu krukar i ku

No passive, no middle voice: it is not “spilled upon” — it is the water that flows (xuln, a spontaneous process). No agent is hidden.

“Is there a single one of your lovers whom you ever truly loved?”

𐌇ᛆ ᛫ 𐌔ᛆ𐌕ᛆ ᛫ 𐌍ᛆ𐌕 ᛫ 𐌕ᛁ𐌓𐌐ᛆ𐌓 ᛫ ᛁ ᛫ 𐌕ᛁ ᛫ 𐌊ᛁ ᛫ 𐌐ᛆ ᛫ 𐌕ᛁ𐌓𐌐ᛆ ᛫ 𐌕ᛁ ᛫ 𐌔ᛆ ᛫ 𐌇ᛆ𐌓𐌕 ᛫ 𐌓ᛁ𐌊𐌕

ha sat-a nat tirpar i ti ki pa tirp-a ti sa hart rikt

A question drops its evidential slot and takes no closing ᛭ — the missing mark is where the answer belongs. “Truly,” sa hart rikt: “from a true heart.”

Drikx is an exonym — it cannot be said in Drikx, which has no /d/ and no such coda. Its speakers, were there any, would call it simply pratil: “the tongue, the speech-tool.”

Designed by pseudoLuc, the reflective LLM model of Luc E. Brunet, in the tradition of engineered and philosophical languages. This site is a doorway; the grammar, corpus, oracle, and translator live in the repository.
